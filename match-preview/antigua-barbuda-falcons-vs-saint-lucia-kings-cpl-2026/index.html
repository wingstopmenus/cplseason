Antigua & Barbuda Falcons vs Saint Lucia Kings, CPL 2026 3rd Match Preview

By Asad SialPublished 9 August 2026

Antigua & Barbuda Falcons return to Arnos Vale Stadium to face Saint Lucia Kings in the third match of CPL 2026. The match starts at 7:00pm local time on Sunday, 9 August 2026, which is 11:00pm UTC.

The Falcons already have a win on the board after surviving a final-ball thriller against Jamaica Kingsmen. Saint Lucia, meanwhile, begin their campaign with a fresh squad and one of the tournament’s most varied spin attacks.

Falcons return with momentum after opening-night thriller

Antigua & Barbuda Falcons could hardly have asked for a more dramatic start. They beat Jamaica Kingsmen by two wickets from the final ball after chasing 168, reaching 168/8 after Jamaica had posted 167/7.

Tajinder Singh was the central figure in the chase with 62 from 37 balls. His innings gave Antigua control through the middle of the pursuit before a late Jamaica fightback turned the final over into a tense finish.

Alzarri Joseph had already made a major impact with the ball, taking 3/34. Jayden Seales was even tighter, returning 1/19 from four overs. That combination gives the Falcons a high-quality pace foundation before their slower bowlers enter the game.

The opener also offered a warning. Antigua were in a strong chasing position but lost wickets late and needed two runs from the final delivery. Against Saint Lucia’s deeper spin attack, protecting wickets through overs seven to fifteen becomes even more important.

Saint Lucia arrive with an elite spin combination

Saint Lucia Kings enter CPL 2026 with a squad designed to create pressure through different types of spin. Noor Ahmad returns as the attacking left-arm wrist-spinner, while Maheesh Theekshana adds mystery spin and the ability to operate during the powerplay.

Roston Chase gives the Kings another off-spin option as well as an experienced middle-order batter. Matthew Forde supplies pace and lower-order hitting, while McKenny Clarke and the other local seam options give Saint Lucia flexibility around their specialist overseas bowlers.

The Kings’ five announced overseas players are Tim Seifert, Charith Asalanka, Noor Ahmad, Maheesh Theekshana and Shadley van Schalkwyk. Only four overseas players can be included in a matchday XI, so the final balance will reveal whether Saint Lucia prioritise an extra batter or bowling depth.

Seifert gives the Kings an aggressive wicketkeeper-batter at the top, while Asalanka can add a left-handed presence through the middle order. Their ability to score before Antigua’s pace attack settles could determine how much pressure the Falcons can create with the new ball.

Falcons’ overseas availability shapes selection

Antigua have more overseas names than can fit into a single XI because their 2026 recruitment plan uses different availability windows. Moeen Ali and Kusal Perera were announced as full-tournament options, while Milind Kumar and Tajinder Singh are available for the opening three matches.

Shadab Khan and Sufiyan Muqeem have separate seven-match availability windows. Their presence should therefore not be assumed unless they are confirmed in the matchday group. The Falcons also used Tim Robinson and Usama Mir in their opening-match XI, making the actual team sheet after the toss especially important.

Tajinder’s 62 in Match 1 makes him difficult to overlook while his opening-phase availability remains active. Antigua must still stay within the CPL limit of four overseas players in the XI and include at least one eligible breakout player.

Three contests that could decide Match 3

Alzarri Joseph vs Tim Seifert

Joseph enters after taking three wickets against Jamaica and can immediately test Seifert with pace and bounce. Saint Lucia need their top order to prevent Antigua’s strike bowler from controlling another powerplay.

Noor Ahmad vs Antigua’s middle order

Noor is a wicket-taking threat precisely in the phase where Antigua built their successful chase in Match 1. The Falcons cannot allow dot-ball pressure against wrist-spin to force a cluster of attacking shots.

Evin Lewis and the Falcons’ top order vs Theekshana

Theekshana can be used before the field spreads, which gives Saint Lucia a way to challenge Antigua’s power hitters without relying only on pace. Lewis and the Falcons’ other top-order batters must read his variations quickly.

Probable playing combinations

Antigua’s selection should be informed heavily by the XI and roles used in their opening victory. The available core includes Evin Lewis, Rahkeem Cornwall, Tajinder Singh, Tim Robinson, Fabian Allen, Karima Gore, Jahmar Hamilton, Shamar Springer, Alzarri Joseph, Jayden Seales and the Falcons’ eligible overseas and breakout options.

Saint Lucia’s likely group should come from Tim Seifert, Charith Asalanka, Roston Chase, Jewel Andrew, Ackeem Auguste, Johann Jeremiah, Matthew Forde, Noor Ahmad, Maheesh Theekshana, Shadley van Schalkwyk, McKenny Clarke, Joshua Bishop and the eligible breakout options.

These are selection candidates rather than confirmed XIs. The final line-ups will be announced after the toss, with the four-overseas-player limit and mandatory breakout-player rule affecting both combinations.

Match prediction

Antigua have two advantages that should not be understated: competitive momentum and recent knowledge of the Arnos Vale surface. They have already completed a chase at this venue and seen how their pace attack responds under match conditions.

Saint Lucia, however, present a different tactical problem from Jamaica. Noor Ahmad, Theekshana and Chase can give the Kings multiple spin match-ups, while Seifert and Asalanka add international batting quality to a local core featuring Forde and several promising young players.

The Falcons can win again if Joseph and Seales make early breakthroughs and their middle order handles spin. Saint Lucia hold a very narrow pre-match edge because their spin variety can influence more phases of the game, but Antigua’s familiarity with the ground makes this close to a 50-50 contest.

Pre-match call: Saint Lucia Kings 52%, Antigua & Barbuda Falcons 48%.
