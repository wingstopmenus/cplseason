Jamaica Kingsmen vs Barbados Tridents, CPL 2026 4th Match Preview

By Asad SialPublished 9 August 2026

Jamaica Kingsmen return to Arnos Vale Stadium to face Barbados Tridents in Match 4 of the Republic Bank Caribbean Premier League 2026. The match starts at 7:00pm local time on Tuesday, 11 August 2026, which is 11:00pm UTC.

Jamaica are looking for their first win after losing a last-ball thriller to Antigua & Barbuda Falcons in the tournament opener. Barbados begin their campaign with a rebranded Tridents side led by Chris Green and strengthened by a high-quality overseas group.

Jamaica need a cleaner middle-overs performance

The Kingsmen came within one delivery of opening their first CPL season with a victory. They made 167/7 against Antigua & Barbuda Falcons before the Falcons reached 168/8 from the final ball of the chase.

Jamaica showed enough in that match to suggest their batting can be dangerous, but the innings lost momentum through the middle overs. That matters against Barbados because Chris Green, Mujeeb Ur Rahman and George Linde give the Tridents several ways to slow the scoring rate and attack favourable match-ups.

Rovman Powell and Andre Russell remain Jamaica’s major power options, while Keacy Carty, Reeza Hendricks, Shayan Jahangir, Shaqkere Parris and Kirk McKenzie provide alternatives higher in the order. The priority is to preserve enough wickets for Powell and Russell to attack rather than forcing them to rebuild.

Jamaica’s overseas availability remains phased. Hassan Khan, Hunain Shah, Shayan Jahangir and Tayyab Arif form the announced opening-phase group, while Saim Ayub, Maaz Sadaqat and Usman Khan are scheduled to join from 19 August. Those later arrivals therefore should not be treated as automatic Match 4 selections.

Barbados bring back the Tridents with a new-look squad

The Barbados Tridents name returns to the CPL in 2026, and the squad has been built around a versatile bowling core. Chris Green leads the side and offers control with off-spin, while Mujeeb Ur Rahman, George Linde and Daniel Sams give Barbados different options across the powerplay, middle overs and death.

The announced overseas contingent features Quinton de Kock, Chris Green, Mujeeb Ur Rahman, George Linde and Daniel Sams. CPL rules allow a maximum of four overseas players in the matchday XI, so Barbados must leave at least one of those five out whenever all are available.

The local group gives the Tridents additional batting and bowling depth. Brandon King can set the tempo at the top, Sherfane Rutherford supplies left-handed power, while Gudakesh Motie and Ramon Simmonds add further bowling options.

Because this is Barbados’ first match of the season, their exact batting order and overseas combination remain less certain than Jamaica’s. The toss and final team sheet will reveal whether the Tridents prioritise an extra specialist batter or load the XI with all-round bowling depth.

Three contests that could decide Match 4

Andre Russell vs Barbados’ spin attack

Russell is most dangerous when he reaches the final overs with freedom to attack. Barbados can counter by forcing him to begin against Green, Mujeeb or Linde, making strike rotation and boundary selection as important as raw power.

Brandon King vs Jamaica’s new ball

King can immediately put pressure on a bowling attack if he finds timing in the powerplay. Hunain Shah, Odean Smith and Jamaica’s other seam options need to deny him the width and length that allow him to score square of the wicket.

Rovman Powell vs Daniel Sams at the death

Powell’s ability to clear the boundary makes him central to Jamaica’s finishing plan. Sams offers left-arm variation and experience at the death, creating a match-up that could decide the final three or four overs of Jamaica’s innings.

Players to watch

Andre Russell

Jamaica’s most explosive finisher can influence the match with both late-order hitting and short spells with the ball.

Rovman Powell

The captain carries a major share of Jamaica’s middle-order responsibility and must manage the innings around the team’s power hitters.

Chris Green

Barbados’ captain can control the scoring rate with off-spin while also giving the Tridents useful lower-order batting.

Brandon King

An attacking top-order batter whose powerplay tempo can determine whether Barbados control the match from the start.

Mujeeb Ur Rahman

His ability to bowl inside the powerplay and through the middle overs gives Barbados a specialist match-up option against Jamaica’s hitters.

Probable playing combinations

Jamaica’s Match 4 selection pool should come from Rovman Powell, Andre Russell, Reeza Hendricks, Keacy Carty, Kirk McKenzie, Shayan Jahangir, Shaqkere Parris, Keemo Paul, Odean Smith, Hassan Khan, Hunain Shah, Jeavor Royal, Vitel Lawes, Jediah Blades and the eligible breakout options.

Barbados’ available squad includes Chris Green, Quinton de Kock, Mujeeb Ur Rahman, George Linde, Daniel Sams, Brandon King, Sherfane Rutherford, Gudakesh Motie, Kadeem Alleyne, Johann Layne, Ramon Simmonds, Rivaldo Clarke, Zachary Carter and their other local and breakout selections.

These are selection candidates rather than confirmed XIs. Both teams will announce their final line-ups after the toss, and the CPL limit of four overseas players plus the breakout-player requirement will affect the final combinations.

Match prediction

Jamaica have already played a competitive match at Arnos Vale and were one delivery away from winning it. That familiarity is valuable, and the Kingsmen possess enough finishing power through Powell, Russell, Keemo Paul and Odean Smith to punish Barbados if wickets are preserved.

The Tridents, however, appear especially well equipped for the middle overs. Green, Mujeeb and Linde can create pressure through different angles and styles, while Sams gives the attack a left-arm seam option. Barbados also have experienced top-order power if de Kock and King are both part of the final XI.

This should be another close Arnos Vale contest. Jamaica’s match experience at the venue keeps the gap narrow, but Barbados’ bowling variety gives them a slight pre-match advantage if their first-choice overseas combination is available.

Pre-match call: Barbados Tridents 53%, Jamaica Kingsmen 47%.
